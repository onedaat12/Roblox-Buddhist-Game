# Story notes

The game follows the traditional account of the Buddha's awakening, mainly as told in the
Pali Canon and its commentaries. This page lists where each part comes from and where the
game takes liberties, so that the story can be checked and corrected.

These notes were written from memory of the traditional sources, not checked line by line
against the texts. If accuracy matters for something you publish, please have someone who
knows the texts read `src/shared/Story.lua`.

## Where the game takes liberties

- **You are not the Buddha.** The player is a seeker walking the same road. People from the
  story (Channa, Sujata, Sotthiya, the two teachers) are met along the way as if in their own time.
- **Temples, stupas and robes did not exist yet.** There were no Buddhist temples before the
  Buddha. The home temple, the shrines, the Mahabodhi temple beside the tree, and robes from
  later traditions (Tibetan, Chan, Zen) are there for atmosphere and to show the wider Buddhist world.
- **Nobody fights in the sources.** The Bodhisatta did not strike Mara's soldiers; he sat
  unmoved. The game needs something to do, so shadows are "dispelled" and turn to petals, and
  sitting in meditation is what makes Mara's storms and discus harmless.
- **The staff, mala and singing bowl** are gifts in the game (from Channa, Alara and the shops).
  The sources do not say this. In the old story a god offers the robe and bowl at the Anoma.
- **Mara's daughters come before the awakening here.** In the Pali texts they come afterwards;
  Sanskrit tellings such as the Buddhacarita and Lalitavistara place them before. The game says so on screen.
- **Mucalinda's pool** is on the road before the tree, though the event happened in the weeks after the awakening. The villager beside it says so.
- **The earth answering.** Pali sources have the earth itself roar. Southeast Asian tradition
  adds an earth goddess who wrings water from her hair. The game uses the first version.
- **Merit as money.** Monastics do not handle money, so shops take merit. Merit is not really spent like this.
- **Names.** Upali, Visakha, Khema, Sona, Tissa and Bhallika are borrowed from well-known
  early disciples and lay followers and given to villagers. They are not those historical people.
- **Geography is compressed.** The real journey from Kapilavastu to Bodh Gaya is hundreds of kilometres.
